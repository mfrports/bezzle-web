Bezzle is a design app for your Mac: apps, websites, social posts and print, in one window. It is free, with every feature included, and needs no account. This page takes you from the download to your first design, and through every part of the window, so the rest of the documentation makes sense wherever you start reading.

## Download and install {#install}

Bezzle comes from its website only, at [bezzle.ch](https://bezzle.ch/download.html). It runs on any Mac with macOS 26 or later, and every copy is signed and notarized by Apple.

::: steps
1. **Open the download.** Double-click the zip file in your Downloads folder, if your browser has not opened it already.
2. **Move it to Applications.** Drag Bezzle into the Applications folder.
3. **Open Bezzle.** From Applications or with Spotlight. Apple has checked it, so it opens like any app.
:::

Bezzle keeps itself up to date. To look for a new version right away, choose {Bezzle › Check for Updates…}. How updates arrive, and where Bezzle keeps your files, is on [Files, updates and privacy](/docs/files.html#updates).

## The welcome tour {#welcome}

The first time Bezzle starts with no designs, it makes a design called {Welcome to Bezzle} in your designs folder and opens it. It is a short tour laid out on the canvas as sheets, one below another. The first sheet says hello and shows how to get about: {{⌘0}} to see every sheet at once, {{⌘=}} and {{⌘-}} (or a pinch) to zoom, {{Space}} held while you drag (or just a scroll) to move around, and {{⌘Z}} to undo anything, as often as you like.

Each sheet after it is a lesson of about a minute: what to try in two or three numbered steps down its left, and the real thing to try it on at its right, marked up with notes in blue pencil. The words you read are locked, so a click goes past them; the things to try are free to move, change and break. Every sheet is a real design, and {{⌘Z}} puts it all back.

The tour is made only that once. If you throw it away it does not come back by itself, but {Help › Welcome to Bezzle} makes a fresh copy whenever you want one, in the language Bezzle is speaking at the time.

## Home {#home}

Home is the first tab of Bezzle's one window, the one named {Bezzle} at the left of the bar along the top. It is where you start designs and find the ones you have. Press {{⌘N}} ({File › New…}) or click {New Design}, the plus after the last tab, to come back to it from a design.

::: figure home
The home screen: sizes standing on their table at one scale, and your designs under them.
:::

While home is the only tab, the window keeps one size, just large enough for the page. The first design you open makes room for itself: the window grows to all of the screen the menu bar and the Dock leave free, in the middle of the screen, but no larger than 2560 by 1440 points on a very large display. From then on you can make the window any size, down to 1000 by 640 points. When you close the last design, the window goes back to home's size.

### Starting a design {#start-a-design}

Under the greeting, which asks what you are designing today, lies a table with every family of sizes along its top edge. The families are grouped in three, with a hairline between them:

- **Devices**: {iPhone}, {Android}, {iPad}, {Mac}, {Web} and {Apple Watch}.
- **Social media**: {Instagram}, {TikTok}, {YouTube}, {Facebook}, {LinkedIn}, {X}, {Pinterest} and {Carousels}.
- **Print & slides**: {Print} and {Slides}.

Last comes {Your own size}. Click a family and its sizes stand up on the table side by side, each drawn as the thing it is for (a phone, a laptop, a story, a sheet of A4), all at one scale, so they are as large beside each other as they really are. The name and the width and height are written under each. Point at one and it lifts toward you; click it to start a design that size.

A new design is called {Untitled} ({Untitled 2}, {Untitled 3} and so on when the name is taken), lies in your designs folder, and has one artboard named after the size you picked, such as {iPhone 18 Pro}. A carousel format starts a carousel of three slides, named {Carousel}. Devices are measured in points, as their screens are drawn; social formats in pixels, as the platforms ask for them; and paper in points, as a PDF has them. All the sizes are listed on [The canvas](/docs/canvas.html#presets).

{Your own size} puts a single sheet on the table. Drag the handle at its top right corner to pull it to any size: it grows from the middle of its foot, in whole tens (in ones for a small size), and the size shows under it as you pull. Or type the width and height under it, from 1 to 100,000. Click the sheet, or press {{Return}} in either number, to start a design that size with one artboard named {Artboard}.

Bezzle remembers which family you had open and your own size, and shows them again next time.

### Your designs {#your-designs}

Under the table, {Your designs} shows every design in your designs folder, and every design you opened from somewhere else, latest first, with how many there are beside the heading. Each card shows the design as it lies on the canvas (the whole of it, or for a large one its largest artboard, with the rest running off the edges), its name, whether it is shared and how, and when it was last changed. Click a card to open the design in a tab of its own; a design that is open already just comes to the front.

Right-click a card for:

::: fields
- **Open**: opens it, as a click does.
- **Show in Finder**: shows its file in a Finder window.
- **Rename**: types a new name in place, under the card. {{Return}} or a click elsewhere keeps it, {{Esc}} leaves the name as it was. The file is renamed too. A design someone shared with you keeps the name it was shared under.
- **Duplicate**: puts a copy named "… copy" next to it. A copy is a design of its own and is never shared.
- **Move to Trash**: for a design in your designs folder. If you shared it, Bezzle asks first, as the link stops working; everyone who has it keeps it as it is, read only.
- **Remove from List**: for a design kept somewhere else. It leaves the home screen, and the file stays where it is.
:::

When there are designs, a {Search designs} field appears in the bar at the top. Type part of a name to show only the designs whose names hold it, whatever the case; {{Esc}} clears it. Beside the heading, the name of your designs folder opens a menu to {Show in Finder} or {Choose Folder…}, the same as {Bezzle › Designs Folder…}. Choosing another folder, and moving your designs there, is explained on [Files, updates and privacy](/docs/files.html#choose-folder).

### Opening files {#opening}

On home, the bar along the top has {Open…} ({{⌘O}}), which opens Bezzle designs and also Adobe XD, Figma, Sketch and Photoshop files, and {From a Website…} ({{⌥⇧⌘I}}), which brings a web page in as a design. You can also drop files anywhere on the home screen ({Drop to open} shows while you hold them over it), or drop a link from your browser to bring that page in. Before you have any designs, the first card of the row is an {Open…} card that does the same as the button.

A file from another app is never changed: each time you open one, Bezzle makes a new design from it in your designs folder. How each app's files come in is on [Bringing designs in](/docs/import.html#ways-in).

## Your first design {#first-design}

::: steps
1. **Pick a size.** On home, click {iPhone}, then {iPhone 18 Pro}. A new design opens in a tab, showing its one artboard whole.
2. **Draw a rectangle.** Press {{R}} and drag on the artboard. Let go, and the {Select} tool is back in your hand with the rectangle selected; its cards show in the inspector on the right.
3. **Change how it looks.** Click its {Fill} card in the inspector to open it, then click the color to pick another.
4. **Add some words.** Press {{T}}, click on the artboard and type. Press {{Esc}} to stop typing; the Text tool stays in your hand for the next text, and a second {{Esc}} puts it down.
5. **Name the design.** Double-click its tab, type a name and press {{Return}}. The file in your designs folder takes the same name.
6. **Close it.** Press {{⌘W}}. There is nothing to save: the design has been on disk since your first change, and it waits on home.
:::

## A tour of the window {#window}

A design in front fills the window in five parts: the bar along the top, the tool rail down the left, the layers (or resources) beside it, the canvas in the middle, and the inspector on the right.

::: figure hero
A design open in Bezzle: tools and layers on the left, the canvas, and the inspector's cards on the right.
:::

### The tab bar {#tab-bar}

The left of the bar along the top holds the {Bezzle} tab for home, a tab for each open design, and a plus, {New Design} ({{⌘N}}), that takes you home to start another. The tab in front rests on a card of its own, at its full width; the others are as wide as their names, and when there are too many for the room, the longest names give way first. A shared design's tab has a small mark before its name: two people while it is shared, a lock once it is no longer shared. More about tabs is in [Tabs](#tabs).

> [!windows] The Bezzle menu
> Bezzle for Windows has no menu bar: the Bezzle menu hangs from the {Bezzle} tab. Click the tab while home is in front, or right-click it from anywhere. It holds {Bring In a Design…} ({{Ctrl+Shift+I}}) for files from XD, Figma, Sketch and Photoshop, {Import from a Website…} ({{Ctrl+Alt+Shift+I}}), {Allow AI Control}, {Copy Claude Code Command}, {Copy Codex Command}, {Copy Server Address} and {Designs Folder…}; with a design in front, also its spelling and {Show in Explorer}. What a Mac keeps in its File, Edit, Object and Format menus is in the window itself: in the right-click menu on the canvas or on a tab, in the top bar and on the tool rail, most of it with the keys on [Keyboard shortcuts](/docs/shortcuts.html).

### The top bar {#top-bar}

The right of the bar holds what there is to do with the design in front, from left to right:

::: fields
- **People**: while others have the design open with you, a token for each of them, which opens into their name as you point at it and takes you to where they are when you click it; otherwise, for a shared design, a tag saying how it is shared, such as {Shared} or {Shared by …}. See [Sharing and working together](/docs/sharing.html#who).
- **Zoom**: the zoom, in percent. Click it for {Zoom In}, {Zoom Out}, {Zoom to Fit}, {Zoom to Selection}, 50%, 100%, 200% and 400%, and for the drawing aids: {Rulers}, {Guides}, {Layout Grids}, {Lock Guides} and {Remove Guides}. See [The canvas](/docs/canvas.html#zoom).
- **Versions**: the clock, {{⌥⌘T}}, brings the ruler of time out along the foot of the canvas. See [Notes and versions](/docs/notes.html#ruler).
- **Share…**: shares the design by link, or once it is shared, shows that it is. For a design that can only be read, it is {Save a Copy} instead. See [Sharing](/docs/sharing.html#by-link).
- **Design**, **Prototype**, **Inspect**: the mode switch. See [The three modes](#modes).
- **Export…**: opens the export panel, {{⌘E}}. In Prototype it turns into {Play}, which plays the prototype, {{⌘Return}}.
:::

A double-click on an empty part of the bar does what a double-click on a title bar does on your Mac, as set in System Settings: usually the window fills the screen, and a second double-click gives it back its size. The window's red close button closes every design in it, each saved, and leaves home; on home, it quits Bezzle.

### The tool rail {#tools}

Down the left are the nine tools. Click one, or press its key while the canvas has the keyboard; point at one to see its name and key.

| Tool | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| {Select} | {{V}} | Picks, moves, resizes and turns layers. |
| {Artboard} | {{A}} | Draws an artboard, or adds one of a preset size. |
| {Rectangle} | {{R}} | Draws rectangles and squares. |
| {Ellipse} | {{E}} | Draws ellipses and circles. |
| {Line} | {{L}} | Draws straight lines. |
| {Pen} | {{P}} | Draws any outline, point by point. |
| {Knife} | {{K}} | Cuts shapes apart along a line. |
| {Text} | {{T}} | Puts down a line of text, or a box for a paragraph. |
| {Note} | {{N}} | Writes a note in the margin about a spot or a part of the design. |

After drawing with any tool but the Text tool, the {Select} tool is back in your hand. {{Esc}} puts the tool down and picks {Select} again. In Prototype and Inspect nothing is drawn, so the tools step aside until you come back to Design.

At the foot of the rail are two switches: {Resources} ({{⇧⌘Y}}) and {Layers} ({{⌘Y}}). They share one place beside the rail, so showing one hides the other, and when the resources go away, the layers come back if they were out before. The same commands are {Show Resources} and {Show Layers} in the menu bar.

### Layers {#layers}

The layers list shows every artboard with what lies on it, and the loose layers on the canvas, topmost first, with the number of layers at the top. Once you select something on an artboard, the list turns to a page of that artboard's layers alone, with its name beside {Layers} at the top; click {Layers} to see every artboard again, or click the artboard's name there to select the artboard.

::: fields
- **Click**: selects the layer and shows it on the canvas. {{⇧}}-click selects every layer between it and the last one you clicked, among the same artboard's or group's layers; {{⌘}}-click adds a layer or takes it away.
- **Double-click**: renames the layer in place. {{Return}} keeps the name, {{Esc}} leaves it.
- **Drag**: moves a layer up or down the list, which moves it in front of or behind the others, or onto a group's or an artboard's row to put it in there.
- **Arrow**: opens or shuts an artboard or a group. Artboards are open until you shut them, groups shut until you open them; a design with more than 12 artboards starts with them all shut.
- **Lock** and **eye**: show while you point at a row, or always while the layer is locked or hidden. Click them to lock or unlock, hide or show the layer.
- **Right-click**: {Rename}, {Edit Text} for a text, {Select Layers Inside} for a group or artboard, {Zoom to Selection}, and then everything the canvas's own menu offers for the selection.
:::

A design with nothing in it yet shows dashed rows where the layers will come, each with a tool and its key: click one to pick that tool. Hidden layers can only be picked here, and locked ones can be picked here though not on the canvas. More on the canvas side of all this is on [The canvas](/docs/canvas.html#lock-hide).

### The canvas {#canvas}

In the middle, the canvas is a table pressed into the page, and your artboards lie on it, each with its name above its top left corner. Layers can lie on an artboard or loose on the canvas beside them. Everything about moving around, selecting, moving, sizing, rulers, guides and snapping is on [The canvas](/docs/canvas.html).

### The inspector {#inspector}

On the right, the inspector shows everything about the selected layers that can be set, and nothing that does not apply to them, as a stack of cards: where they are first ({Frame}), then what they are (such as {Text} or {Artboard}), then how they look ({Fill}, {Border}, {Shadow} and the rest).

- **Folded**, a card is one line: a small live picture, its name, and what it comes to, such as `402 × 874 · 0, 0`. Pointing at a card outlines on the canvas the part of the selection it changes.
- **Click** a card to open it, and again to fold it. One card is open at a time; {Frame} is open to begin with.
- **Drag sideways** across a folded card to set its amount, such as how much a shadow lifts the layer: the card's width is the whole range, it fills with pencil blue as you go, and it clicks to the presets as it passes them, with a tick of the trackpad. Drag far enough past the left end of a card that can be taken away and it says {Release to remove}; let go, and it is gone.
- **Point** at a preset to see it on the canvas before you click it.
- **Dashed chips** at the foot offer what the selection could have and has not yet, such as a shadow. Point at one to see it on the canvas, and click to add it, already looking right, with its card open.
- **Several of a kind**, such as two fills, lie in a pile. Drag a card up or down its pile to lay it over or under the others.

With nothing selected, the inspector shows the design's styles and, under them, every tool with its key and {Move around} with {{Space}}. With the Artboard tool in hand, or while the design has no artboards, it shows the artboard sizes to add instead. Every card is explained on the page about what it does, starting with [How the look cards work](/docs/looks.html#look-cards).

### Resources {#resources}

The resources panel holds ready-made parts and icons: the design's own components, the components and styles of your other designs, four kits ({Bezzle UI Kit}, {iOS}, {macOS} and {Android}) and three icon sets (Lucide, Phosphor and Material Symbols). Click one to open it as a page of its own, or type in {Search} to look through all of them at once. Parts are drawn as they would come out on the artboard they would go onto, light or dark. A click puts a part onto the artboard in view; dragging one carries the real thing onto the canvas, where it shows how it will land before you let go. See [The Resources panel](/docs/components.html#resources-panel).

## The three modes {#modes}

The switch at the top right shows the same design three ways. Each part has a small drawing that does what it says as it comes to the front.

::: fields
- **Design**: draw the design. Every tool is in the rail, and the inspector shows the selection's cards.
- **Prototype**: see the design as a prototype, its screens and the ways between them, {{⌃P}}. The tools step aside, the inspector shows where the selected layers go, and {Export…} becomes {Play}. See [Prototype mode](/docs/prototype.html#prototype-mode).
- **Inspect**: inspect the design as it is built, its sizes, distances, looks and code, {{⌃I}}. Nothing in the design changes here, so you can inspect a design shared with you to read. One thing is picked at a time. See [Inspect mode](/docs/inspect.html#inspect-mode).
:::

{{⌃P}} turns Prototype on and off; {{⌃I}} turns Inspect on, and off again back to Prototype. {Play Prototype} ({{⌘Return}}) plays from where the prototype starts, or from the screen you picked. Each tab keeps its own mode.

## Saving without Save {#saving}

There is no Save command. A design is written to disk a moment after every change, on a queue of its own so that a large design never holds you up, and where you were looking is kept too, so a design opens where you left it (unless nothing of it can be seen from there, when it opens with all of it in view). Closing a tab saves the design, keeps a version if it changed, and makes the picture the Finder shows for it.

If you rename or move a design in the Finder while it is open, Bezzle follows it there the next time you come back to Bezzle, and goes on saving it where it now is. If its file disappears while you work, Bezzle writes it again where it was, so nothing you did is lost.

Quitting saves every open design first. If one could not be written, Bezzle says which and why, and offers {Keep Bezzle Open} or {Quit Anyway}. When Bezzle starts again, it opens at home.

### Save a Copy {#save-a-copy}

{File › Save a Copy} ({{⇧⌘S}}) makes a design of its own from the one in front, as it is now, pictures and all: it is called "… copy", goes into your designs folder, and opens in a new tab. For a design someone shared with you and no longer shares, it is how to keep one you can change, and the {Share…} button turns into {Save a Copy} for it.

### Versions {#versions}

Bezzle keeps versions of every design by itself: one after every ten minutes of work, when the design has changed, and one each time you close its tab after a change. {File › Versions} ({{⌥⌘T}}) or the clock in the top bar brings out the ruler of time to look back and go back. See [Versions](/docs/notes.html#versions).

## Tabs {#tabs}

Each open design has a tab of its own, after home. Opening a design that is open already brings its tab to the front.

::: fields
- **Click** a tab to bring it to the front.
- **Drag** a tab sideways to move it along the bar; the others make way.
- **Double-click** a tab to rename the design. {{Return}} or a click elsewhere keeps the new name, {{Esc}} leaves it.
- **×** on the tab in front, or on one you point at, closes it ({Close Tab}, {{⌘W}}).
- **Right-click** a tab for {Rename}, {Show in Finder}, {Close Tab} and {Close Other Tabs}.
:::

{Window › Show Next Tab} ({{⌃Tab}}) and {Window › Show Previous Tab} ({{⌃⇧Tab}}) step through the tabs, home included, going round. A tab you leave keeps its canvas ready, so coming back shows the design at once.

Closing a tab saves the design and lets it go. {File › Close Window} ({{⇧⌘W}}) closes every design, each saved, and leaves home. With home in front, {File › Close} ({{⌘W}}) and {Close Window} quit Bezzle, once everything is saved.

> [!note] Renaming a design renames its file
> A design's name is its file's name. A `/` in a new name becomes a `-`, and if another design in the same folder already has the name, a number is added. Changing only the capitals, such as menu to Menu, adds none.

## Undo {#undo}

{Edit › Undo} ({{⌘Z}}) takes back the last change, and {Edit › Redo} ({{⇧⌘Z}}) does it again, as many steps back as you like. The menu names each step, such as {Undo Move} or {Undo Add Rectangle}.

- **One history per design.** Each tab keeps its own, so Undo in one design never reaches into another.
- **A drag is one step.** Moving, resizing, scrubbing a number or dragging across a card undoes in one go, however long it took. Press {{Esc}} during a drag to put everything back as it was before it began, without a step at all.
- **Typing has its own.** While you type into a text on the canvas, Undo takes back what you just typed; once you stop typing, the whole of it is one step.
- **Shared designs.** When others work on the design with you, Undo takes back only what your step did, and their changes stay.
- **Closing a tab ends its history.** Open the design again and the history starts afresh. To go further back, use [Versions](/docs/notes.html#going-back).

> [!tip] Nothing to lose
> Between Undo and Versions, you can try anything. The welcome tour says it best: move things, change them, break them, and {{⌘Z}} puts it all back.
