Notes are what you and the people you work with write about a design, as a reviewer writes in the margin of a drawing: each thread is pinned to a spot on a layer, or to a part of the design circled in pencil, and hangs on a pencil thread from there to a card on the table beside the artboard, never over the work. Versions are the design as it was at earlier moments, kept by themselves as you work. The ruler of time lays them out along the foot of the canvas, to look back through, to name, and to go back to.

Both are kept inside the design's file, and in a shared design both are shared: everyone's notes, and every version someone names.

::: figure together
Notes in the margin, each on a pencil thread from the spot or the loop it is about, in its writer's color.
:::

## Writing a note {#writing}

::: steps
1. **Take the Note tool.** Press {{N}}, or click {Note} in the tool rail.
2. **Click what the note is about.** A click on a layer pins the note to that spot on it; a click on an artboard's name, to the artboard; a click on empty canvas, to that spot on the canvas. Or draw a loop round part of the design (see below).
3. **Write it.** A card opens beside the spot with a field that says “Write a note”. Type, and press {{Return}} to post it.
:::

Once you have clicked, the {Select} tool is back in your hand. The note is signed with your name, as the others in a shared design know you, and drawn in your pencil: blue on your own Mac.

While you write:

- **{{Return}}** posts the note. The send arrow beside the field does the same; it appears as soon as there is something to send.
- **{{Esc}}** lets the note go unwritten.
- **Clicking elsewhere** posts what you have written. A note with no words is simply let go.
- The field grows as you type, up to eight lines.

### Circling part of the design {#circling}

With the Note tool, press and drag round what you mean, as you would ring something on a printout. The loop shows once the pointer has moved a few points. Let go, and the note is about what you circled. A loop smaller than 10 points across on screen counts as a click, and makes a note at the spot where you pressed. {{Esc}} while drawing puts the loop away.

What a loop is about is worked out from what it is round:

- the largest layer that lies at least 60% inside the loop, unless the loop is more than four times its size;
- otherwise, the smallest layer that holds the whole loop;
- otherwise, the artboard under the middle of the loop;
- and drawn on the canvas away from any artboard, the canvas itself.

A circled note keeps its loop round that layer, at the same place on it, as the layer moves and changes size. Each loop is drawn by hand, a little differently each time, never quite round, its end running on past its start.

A note can't be written in a design that is read only (see [Stop Sharing and read only](/docs/sharing.html#stop-sharing)).

## Where notes show {#placement}

Notes keep off the work. Each thread is a card in the margin beside the artboard it is about (the sheet, as the margin sees it), on the side nearer its spot, level with it where there is room, and the cards make room for one another down the margin in the order of their spots. A pencil thread runs from the card to its pin.

- **Cards** are 236 points wide and sit 26 points out from the artboard's edge, whatever the zoom.
- **Flags**: where the margin has no room for a card, the thread is a flag stuck to the artboard's edge instead, level with its spot, with its writer's initial on it. An artboard shorter than 120 points on screen takes flags only. Many flags on one edge grow smaller and closer together. Zoom in, and flags open out into cards as room appears; Bezzle waits for a little more room than a card needs before it turns one back, so a thread at the edge of having room does not flicker between the two.
- **The open thread** always gets a card. With no room beside its artboard, it lifts over the table.
- **On the canvas itself**: a note about a spot away from any artboard has its card beside the pin, or, with no room for that, just its writer's token on the pin.
- **Pins**: each thread's spot is a small pin in its writer's pencil; a circled note has its loop instead, with the thread leaving the loop on the card's side. No card or flag ever covers a pin, so every pin can always be dragged.

A note pinned to a layer stays at the same spot on it as the layer moves and is resized. Notes show in {Design} mode; in {Prototype} and {Inspect}, and while you look back through versions, they step aside.

## Reading and answering {#threads}

Click a card, a flag or a pin to open its thread. Click the pin again, press {{Esc}}, or select something on the canvas, and it closes. Pointing at a pin or a flag shows its first note as a tag and lights up the layer it is about.

A closed card shows the first three lines of the thread's first note and, if it has answers, the tokens of up to four people who answered and how many answers there are. An open card shows everything, oldest first, each answer below a hairline, and at the foot a field that says “Answer”. Type, and press {{Return}} or click the arrow to send it; the field stays ready for another. {{Esc}} in the field closes the thread.

The head of each note shows its writer's token and name, and how long ago it was written. The head of an open thread also has:

::: fields
- **‹** and **›** ({Previous Note}, {Next Note}): go to the thread before or after, in the order of a review: artboard by artboard from the top row down and left to right along it, down each artboard, and notes on the canvas itself last. They go round from the last to the first, and show while the margin holds more than one thread. The view flies to each, coming closer if the artboard is too small on screen to read, never past actual size.
- **The round mark** ({Mark as Done}): ticks the thread off. See below.
- **A dot** after the name: the thread has words written on another Mac since you last read it here. Flags of threads you have read are paler. What you have read is remembered on each Mac, for each reader.
:::

Words in a note can be selected and copied like any text.

## Moving, editing and ticking off {#managing}

### Moving a note {#moving}

Drag a thread's pin to put it somewhere else. Its thread comes after it, sagging a little, and the card follows. Over another layer the pin catches, with a tap you feel on the trackpad, and let go there, the note is about that layer from then on. Let go on empty canvas, it is about that spot. A circled note moves its loop along, and is about whatever the loop is round where it lands.

### Changing and deleting {#editing}

You can change or delete the notes written on your own Mac:

- **Change the words**: double-click them in an open card, or right-click the thread and choose {Edit}. {{Return}} keeps the change, {{Esc}} leaves the words as they were.
- **Delete an answer**: point at it in the open card and click the trash icon that appears in its head.
- **Delete a thread**: right-click it and choose {Delete}. Its answers go with it.

### Ticking a thread off {#done}

Once a thread is talked out, click the round mark at its head. The tick is drawn in pencil, the card is tucked away and its thread reeled in, and the thread leaves the margin. Anyone can tick a thread off, not only its writer.

Ticked-off threads are not gone. Take the Note tool ({{N}}) and they show again, as flags with a tick on them. Open one, and click its mark again, or choose {Open Again}, to bring it back into the margin.

### The note menu {#note-menu}

Right-click a card, a flag or a pin for:

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| {Answer} | Opens the thread with the typing in its answer field. Not on a ticked-off thread. |
| {Mark as Done}, {Open Again} | Ticks the thread off, or brings it back. |
| {Edit} | Opens the thread to change the words of its first note. Only for notes written on this Mac. |
| {Copy} | Copies the whole thread as text, each note as its writer's name and words, a blank line between them. |
| {Delete} | Deletes the thread with its answers. Only for notes written on this Mac. |

In a design that is read only, the menu offers {Copy} alone.

### Notes and Undo {#notes-undo}

Every change to notes is a step of the design's Undo history, named in the {Edit} menu: {Write Note}, {Answer Note}, {Change Note}, {Move Note}, {Close Note}, {Open Note Again} and {Delete Note}.

When a layer with notes on it is deleted, the notes stay where it was, on the canvas, so nothing written is lost with it. Undo the deletion and they are back on the layer.

## Notes in a shared design {#shared-notes}

A shared design carries everyone's notes. Each note is drawn in its writer's pencil, the same color they have on the canvas, and unread threads show their dot as answers come in from the others.

Notes merge as the rest of a shared design does, every note and every property of it on its own: two people answering at once both keep their answer, and an answer, a change to the words and the thread being ticked off, made at the same moment by different people, all stay. A thread someone deleted while someone else was answering it, who had not seen it go, comes back with its answers, so the answer is not lost.

An AI assistant can read, write and answer notes too. Its notes are signed “Claude” (see [AI assistants](/docs/ai.html#notes-versions)).

## Versions {#versions}

As you work, Bezzle keeps versions of the design by itself: the whole design as it was at that moment, without where it was looked at. You can also name a version, and a named version is kept for good.

A version is taken when the design is saved and:

- it has no version yet;
- or the newest is at least 10 minutes old and the design has changed since;
- or you close its tab and it has changed since the newest.

Versions taken by themselves are thinned out over time, as Time Machine thins backups:

| Age | What is kept |
| --- | --- |
| The last hour | every version |
| The last day | one for each half hour |
| The last month | one a day |
| Older | one a week |

Named versions are always kept, and so is the newest. Versions live inside the design's file, so they go wherever it goes. {Duplicate} on the home screen keeps them in the copy; {File › Save a Copy} starts the copy with none. A design kept as a single file from Bezzle's early days becomes a package the first time it needs room for one (see [What a design file is](/docs/files.html#design-file)).

> [!tip] Undo and versions
> Undo is for the steps you just took, and lasts while the design is open: closing its tab clears its history. Versions reach back over days and weeks, and stay with the design.

## The ruler of time {#ruler}

Open it with {File › Versions} ({{⌥⌘T}}), or the clock button in the top bar. It is a card at the foot of the canvas, up to 600 points wide.

Along it runs a comb of fine strokes, one for each version, oldest at the left, and a circle at the right end for now. Each stroke is the color of the sky when that version was made: blue by day, gold and rose towards evening, indigo at night. Named versions stand taller, with a dot on top in the pencil of whoever named them. Under the first stroke of each day is its name: {Today}, {Yesterday}, the weekday for the last week, and the date before that; the step from one day to the next is a little wider than between versions of the same day.

Point at the comb and the strokes swell under the pointer, as the Dock does, with a label gliding along that names the version nearest: its day, its time and its name.

::: fields
- **Click a stroke**: the canvas shows the design as it was then. The pencil line flies there, plucking each stroke it passes.
- **Drag along the comb**: the line lifts and follows the pointer, showing each version as it passes, and settles on the one under it when you let go.
- **Two fingers across the card**, or the scroll wheel: one version for each stretch, back or forward.
- **{{←}}** and **{{→}}**: the version before or after; past the newest, now. At now, the arrows step through versions only while nothing is selected, so they still nudge a selection.
- **{{Esc}}**, or the ✕ at the card's right: puts the ruler away and brings you back to now.
- **The play button** ({Play its story}): plays the design's making, from its first version to now, as the light of the hours it was made in passes over it. A long history plays some thirty steps, every named version among them, in about ten seconds. Click again to {Stop}.
:::

The head of the card names what the canvas shows: {Now}, or the version's day and time, such as “Today, 14:32”, with its name beside it if it has one.

## Looking back {#looking-back}

Going from one version to another, the layers glide to how they were: those not made yet fade away, and those taken away since come back. Then what differs from the design as it is now is marked:

- **Changed since**: pencil crop marks at its corners.
- **Gone since**: the same marks in sepia.
- **Made since**: a faint dotted outline where it will be.
- **Pointing at a changed layer**: it shows, faintly, where it is now, with a pencil thread from where it was to there.

Looking back changes nothing. The inspector is dimmed, and any change made meanwhile, from a menu, by Undo or by an assistant, brings the design as it is now back into view so you see the change as it is made. In a shared design, changes others make while you look back come in as usual, and the marks are worked out again against them.

## Going back {#going-back}

### The whole design {#whole}

With a version on the canvas, click {Go Back to This Version}. The design becomes what it was then. Before that, the design as it is now is kept as a named version, called “Before going back to” and the version's name or time, so nothing is lost by it. Its notes stay as they are now, and so does where you are looking. Going back is a step of the Undo history, {Go Back to Version}, which {{⌘Z}} takes back. If the design is already as it was then, nothing happens.

### One layer, or a few {#bring-back}

Click a layer that is marked as differing to pick it; a small round tick at its corner shows it is picked, and a second click lets it go again. {Bring Back} appears in the card's head. Click it, and the picked layers come back as they were into the design as it is now: each where it is now, or, if it has gone since, where it was, with everything in it. The layers brought back are selected, and their corners mark them landing. The step is {Bring Back Layer}, or {Bring Back Layers}, in the Undo history.

In a design that is read only you can look back, but not go back or bring anything back.

## Naming versions {#naming}

Click the flag in the card's head: at now it offers to {Name this moment}, at a version to {Give it a name}. Type a name and press {{Return}}; {{Esc}} leaves it unnamed. Given no words, the version is called “Named version”.

To rename a named version, click its name. Clear the name to make it an ordinary version again, thinned out in time like the others. Naming the moment now, or a version that was taken by itself, keeps it for good.

## Versions in a shared design {#shared-versions}

The versions Bezzle takes by itself stay on each Mac: everyone has their own. A version someone names while the design is shared goes to everyone, with the design as it was then, and appears on everyone's ruler, its dot in its namer's pencil. To the others in the design at that moment it goes at once, when it is small enough; otherwise, and to everyone else, through Bezzle's server or the shared folder. Versions named before the design was shared stay on your Mac.

In a design kept in a shared folder, each Mac keeps its own list of versions in the design, so two Macs never write over each other's list, and each sees its own versions and those everyone named.

An assistant can list, name and go back to versions, and show one on your canvas with the ruler out (see [AI assistants](/docs/ai.html#notes-versions)).
